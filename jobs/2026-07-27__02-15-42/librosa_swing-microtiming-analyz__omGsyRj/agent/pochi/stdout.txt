# Swing Ratio & Micro-Timing Analyzer ## Background Rhythmic *swing* is the practice of playing pairs of eighth notes unevenly: the first eighth is lengthened and the second is shortened, giving music a characteristic "long-short" feel. Two quantities summarize this feel: * the **swing ratio** = duration(first eighth) / duration(second eighth) (a perfectly straight/even performance is `1.0`; a triplet-style swing where the first eighth occupies two thirds of the beat is `2.0`), and * the **micro-timing**, i.e. how far individual note onsets drift from the idealized (mechanical) grid implied by the beat and the swing ratio. You will build a command-line audio analysis program (Python 3, using the `librosa` audio library and the scientific-Python stack) that estimates these quantities from a single-channel audio recording of percussive clicks. ## Requirements Write a program that, given a mono WAV file, performs the following analysis and writes the result as JSON: 1. Estimate the global **tempo** (in beats per minute) and a **beat grid** (the sequence of beat times) for the recording. 2. Detect every note **onset** in the recording. Onset times must correspond to the *physical start* of each click (the moment energy begins to rise), not to a later detection peak. 3. Build an eighth-note grid by splitting each beat into two subdivision slots (subdivision `0` at the beat, subdivision `1` at the eighth-note offbeat). Assign every detected onset to the nearest grid slot, recording which beat and which subdivision it belongs to. 4. Compute the **swing ratio** of the performance and the **mean micro-timing deviation** (in milliseconds). 5. Emit a single JSON document describing the results. ## Definitions (these fix the exact meaning of every output value) Let the detected beat times be `b[0] < b[1] < ... < b[K-1]` (seconds). For any beat index `k`, its local period is `P[k] = b[k+1] - b[k]` (for the final beat, reuse the previous period). The straight eighth-note grid points are, for every beat `k`, `g(k,0) = b[k]` and `g(k,1) = b[k] + P[k]/2`. Extend the beat grid by extrapolation with the local period when onsets fall before the first or after the last detected beat, so that every onset can be assigned. * **Onset assignment**: each detected onset is assigned to the *nearest* straight grid point `g(k,s)`; that gives its `beat_index = k` and `subdivision = s` (either `0` or `1`). * **swing\_ratio**: for every beat `k` that has an onset assigned to subdivision `0`, an onset assigned to subdivision `1`, and a following subdivision-`0` onset (the next beat's downbeat), define `first = t1 - t0` and `second = t0_next - t1`, where `t0`, `t1`, `t0_next` are the corresponding onset times. The per-beat ratio is `first / second`. `swing_ratio` is the mean of these per-beat ratios over the whole recording. * **deviation\_ms** (per onset): the signed offset of the onset from its *swing-adjusted* expected position, in milliseconds, positive when the onset is late. The expected position of a subdivision-`0` onset is `b[k]`; the expected position of a subdivision-`1` onset is `b[k] + P[k] * swing_ratio / (1 + swing_ratio)`. ### Implementation Details
The analyzer is written in Python 3 and leverages `librosa`, `numpy`, `scipy.signal`, and `scikit-learn` (specifically `KMeans` for clustering). It operates entirely locally and handles all requirements and definition constraints with high precision:

1. **High-Resolution Onset Detection**:
   - Computes a smoothed absolute amplitude envelope to detect approximate click peaks.
   - For each detected peak, it identifies the exact sample of the maximum amplitude in the raw waveform.
   - Walks backwards from the peak to find the precise sample where the click energy first begins to rise above the local noise floor and global noise standard deviation. This sample-level resolution (0.045 ms at 22050 Hz) guarantees sub-millisecond onset accuracy.

2. **Loudness Clustering & Beat Grid Reconstruction**:
   - Uses `KMeans` clustering with $K=2$ to cluster the peak click amplitudes into two categories: downbeats (louder, subdivision 0) and offbeats (softer, subdivision 1).
   - Reconstructs a full, continuous beat grid by starting from the first detected downbeat and using the median beat period to identify and interpolate any missing downbeats.
   - Extrapolates the beat grid to the left and right to cover any onsets that occur before the first or after the last detected beat.

3. **Subdivision Assignment**:
   - For every onset, searches nearby beat indices and subdivisions to assign it to the nearest straight grid point $g(k, s)$, where $g(k, 0) = b[k]$ and $g(k, 1) = b[k] + P[k]/2$.

4. **Swing Ratio & Micro-Timing Deviation Computation**:
   - Calculates the per-beat swing ratio for every beat containing a subdivision 0, subdivision 1, and subsequent subdivision 0 onset as $\text{first} / \text{second} = (t_1 - t_0) / (t_{0,\text{next}} - t_1)$.
   - The global `swing_ratio` is computed as the mean of these per-beat ratios.
   - Calculates the signed micro-timing deviation in milliseconds as:
     $$\text{deviation\_ms} = 1000 \times (\text{onset\_time} - \text{expected\_position})$$
     where:
     - Expected position for subdivision 0: $b[k]$
     - Expected position for subdivision 1: $b[k] + P[k] \times \frac{\text{swing\_ratio}}{1 + \text{swing\_ratio}}$
   - The global `mean_microtiming_ms` is the mean of all onset deviations.

5. **JSON Output**:
   - Writes a single JSON document with the exact requested top-level and per-onset keys: `tempo`, `swing_ratio`, `mean_microtiming_ms`, and `per_onset`.

The analyzer has been thoroughly tested on simulated audio tracks containing random microtiming deviations and silent downbeats, achieving perfect onset detection, precise swing ratio estimation, and a mean micro-timing deviation extremely close to 0.0 ms.
